🌱 Land and Purchase Options

A packager asks one big question: what is the family buying, and is it ready within the loan's time limits?

Common ways a 502 family gets a home

1. Buy an existing homeNeeds an eligible rural location, a purchase agreement, and an appraisal. The home must be modest and meet RD's condition standards.
2. Build on land the family already ownsNeeds clear title, plans and specs, a builder, and a cost breakdown.
3. Buy a lot and buildNeeds a signed contract or option on the site, plus plans, a builder, and a site that has water, sewer or septic, and road access.
4. Buy from a builder or developer (new construction)The sales contract covers the site and the home together.
5. Mutual self-helpSeveral families build together with a nonprofit's help and their own labor.

The 30-day option question

You asked for a list of land purchase options tied to 30 days. Where a family uses an option or contract to hold a site, the term has to give RD time to review and obligate funds. I did not want to hand you a wrong day count from memory.

💰 Income Limits and 🗺️ Eligibility Maps

How income limits work

Limits are not one number per state. They vary by county and by household size, and they are updated by RD.

Income tiers to know Very low: under 50% of area median. Low: 50% to 80%. Moderate: up to 115%, used for guaranteed loans, not 502 Direct.
What counts for 502 DirectAdjusted household income is compared to the low-income limit. You also check repayment ability and whether the family cannot get credit elsewhere on reasonable terms.

Where to look it up

I cannot show every state's limits here because they run to thousands of county rows and change often. RD keeps the current official numbers on its eligibility site, which also covers the property maps.
